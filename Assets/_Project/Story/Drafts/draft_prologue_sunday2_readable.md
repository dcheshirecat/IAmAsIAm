## Drive

Sunday. Maya is eight now. Eight is old enough to know things.

She knows that the car smells like coffee and the papers on the back seat. She knows that if she talks while Mommy is practicing her sermon, Mommy will say "in a minute," and the minute won't come.

She knows that there's a note from Miss Susan in her backpack. It has been in there for nine days.

**Choice:**

- **Show Mommy the note.**
    - Maya holds it up between the seats.
    - **Maya:** Mommy, Miss Susan says you have to sign this. It's for the conference.
    - **Sarah:** Put it on the dashboard, honey. I'll look at it later.
    - Maya puts it on the dashboard. On the way into church it slides off, under the seat, with the other papers.
- **Leave it in your backpack.**
    - It isn't a good time. It's never a good time on Sundays, and on weekdays Mommy is at the church.
    - Maya leaves the note where it is. It's getting soft at the corners.
- **Sign it yourself.**
    - Maya takes out her pencil. She writes "Sarah" on the line, in her neatest grown-up writing, the way it looks on the church bulletin.
    - It doesn't look grown-up. It looks like an eight-year-old wrote it.
    - She folds it up small, so nobody will see.

## First day school

First Day School is in the same room, with the same carpet. Miss Eloise still has the poster of the light under the bushel.

Rebecca still has a purple hairband. She sits with Jamie and Justin and Will now, all the time. She waves at Maya, though. She always waves.

**Rebecca:** Hi, Maya! Do you like my new shoes? My mom got them at the mall.

**Maya:** They're nice.

**Rebecca:** You can sit with us if you want. There's a chair.

There is a chair. It's at the corner, where you can't really see.

**Choice:**

- **Take the chair at the corner.**
    - Maya sits at the corner. The boys are talking about a video game she doesn't have. Rebecca laughs at everything Will says.
    - Maya laughs too, a little bit late, so she'll know when.
- **Sit with Miss Eloise and help.**
    - Maya helps Miss Eloise set out the crayons, one box per table.
    - **Miss Eloise:** You're such a help, Maya. A real little minister's daughter.
    - Maya smiles. That's a good thing to be. She thinks.

## Fellowship

After worship, there are snacks in the hall. Maya is very hungry. She didn't have breakfast. She didn't really have dinner last night, either, just crackers and the end of the peanut butter, because Mommy had the board meeting.

There are cookies. There are little squares of cheese on toothpicks. There are grapes.

Maya takes some. Then some more. She puts some in her pocket, wrapped in a napkin, for later.

**Choice:**

- **Keep eating. Nobody's looking.**
    - Maya eats until her tummy feels tight. It's a good feeling. It means there's enough.
- **Stop. People might notice.**
    - Maya stops. Somebody might see. Mrs. Hines is right there.
    - She keeps the napkin in her pocket, though.

Somebody did see.

Mira is at the end of the table, with her cane and her coffee and her moss cardigan. She doesn't say anything about the napkin. She just moves the plate of cheese a little closer to Maya.

**Mira:** Good morning, Maya. I saved you a chair.

**Choice:**

- **Sit with Mira.**
- **Say you're fine where you are.**
    - **Maya:** I'm okay here.
    - **Mira:** Of course. The chair will wait.
    - Maya stands for a while. Then she sits in the chair anyway. Mira doesn't say "I told you so." She never does.

**Mira:** What did you have for dinner last night?

**Maya:** Crackers. And peanut butter. Mommy had the board meeting.

**Mira:** On your own?

**Maya:** I'm allowed. I lock the door. I know the number for the church.

Mira nods slowly. She has a way of nodding that means she is thinking very hard and doesn't want you to see.

**Mira:** How many evenings is Mommy at meetings, would you say?

**Maya:** Um. Monday. And Wednesday is choir. And Thursday is the board. And sometimes Friday.

**Mira:** That's a lot of crackers.

**Maya:** I like crackers.

## Floaty

The hall is loud. Lots of grown-ups talking, and the coffee machine gurgling, and somebody laughing too high.

And Maya goes floaty.

It happens more now. In the quiet part of worship, and in the car, and sometimes in bed. Like the balloon string gets longer every time.

She's sitting in the chair. She can see her own knees, and the napkin in her lap, and Mira's hand on the table.

**Mira:** Maya?

Somebody is saying her name. It sounds like it's at the bottom of a swimming pool.

**Mira:** Maya, dear?

She should answer. She knows she should answer. But the words are down there, and she's up here.

**Choice:**

- **Try to answer.**
    - Maya tries to make her mouth work. It's like trying to move in a dream.
- **Stay up here. It's quieter.**
    - It's quieter up here. Nobody needs anything up here.

**Mira:** Maya. Feet on the floor, love. Hello, floor.

Hello, floor. Maya remembers. She pushes her feet down, just a little.

**Maya:** ...Hello, floor.

**Mira:** There you are.

Mira's face is very close. It is a worried face trying to be a calm face.

**Mira:** Let's play a game. Tell me five things you can see. Anything at all.

Five things you can see. _(the player does the "five-senses" exercise here)_

**Maya:** The coffee pot. Your cane. A grape on the floor. Mrs. Hines's earrings. The window.

**Mira:** Five. You're very good at that.

**Maya:** Did I go away?

**Mira:** Only a little way. And you came back. That's the important part.

## Mira goes

Mira holds Maya's hand for a moment. Her hand is dry and light, like paper.

Then she looks across the hall, toward the hallway where Mommy's office is. She looks at it for a long time.

**Mira:** Maya, will you stay right here and finish your grapes? I need to have a word with your mother.

**Maya:** Am I in trouble?

**Mira:** No, dear. Not you. Never you.

Mira gets up, slow, with her cane, and goes down the hallway.

At the end of the hallway, by the drinking fountain, Reneé Carter is sitting on the bench with her crutches across her knees, resting. She's in high school now. She looks up when Mira goes past, and then she looks at the floor, very politely, the way you do when you are not listening.

Maya eats her grapes. They take a long time.

## Mira returns

When Mira comes back, she is walking slower than before.

Behind her, Mommy comes out of the office too, already putting on her coat, already talking to Mrs. Hines.

**Mrs. Hines:** Pastor Sarah, the hymnal committee is waiting.

**Sarah:** Coming, coming.

Mommy stops by Maya's chair. She smiles at Mira, a big warm church smile.

**Sarah:** Thank you, Mira. Really. You're so good to her. She's very independent, our Maya.

Then she's gone again, down the other hallway, with Mrs. Hines.

Mira sits down. She doesn't say anything for a while. She picks the grape up off the floor and puts it in a napkin.

**Choice:**

- **Ask Mira what she said to Mommy.**
    - **Maya:** What did you say to Mommy?
    - **Mira:** Oh. Grown-up things. Just talking.
    - She smiles. It's not her real smile. Maya knows the difference now.
- **Don't ask.**
    - Maya doesn't ask. If she asks, Mira might tell her. And then she'd know.

**Mira:** Maya. If you're ever hungry, or ever on your own and frightened, you can call me. Any time at all. Even late.

She writes her number on a napkin, in shaky blue pen, and folds it into Maya's hand.

**Mira:** Will you remember?

**Maya:** I'll remember.

## Home

In the car, Mommy is quiet for a while. Then she's on the phone again.

Maya keeps her hand in her pocket, around the napkin with the number on it.

She never calls it. She thinks about it lots of times. She's afraid it would be making a fuss.

But she keeps it. She keeps it for years.
