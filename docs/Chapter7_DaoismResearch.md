## Who a Daoist priest (daoshi) usually is
- **Zhengyi ("Orthodox Unity")**: priests may marry, live at home, and often serve part-time, running a family or community
  temple and doing rituals for families: blessings, funerals, festivals. Most Daoist priests in Taiwan and the diaspora are Zhengyi.
- **Quanzhen ("Complete Perfection")**: monastic and celibate, living in monasteries; known for meditation and inner cultivation.
- In North America, Daoist practice is mostly found in **community temples** (Daoist and folk deities side by side, often shared with
  Buddhist ones: for example the Seattle Taoist-Buddhist Association's Ling Shen Ching Tze Temple, in Redmond), and in **Daoist
  centres** teaching tai chi, qigong and meditation. Organisations have multiplied since the 1965 Immigration Act.

## What a visitor can do (open practice)
- Light incense the right way (a temple member usually shows you), bow, and make a small offering.
- Join the festivals: **Lunar New Year** and the Jade Emperor's birthday (the 9th day of the 1st lunar month, when people come to
  offer incense); **Qingming** (tending ancestors' graves in spring); **Zhongyuan, the Ghost Festival** (the 15th of the 7th lunar
  month: offerings and repentance for wandering, suffering spirits; often spread across the month).
- Tai chi or qigong in a park at dawn; reading the *Tao Te Ching* (the chapter intro already quotes chapter 8: water).
