# Trigger ideas (DRAFT for the owner to edit)

**Status:** ideas only. Nothing goes into `Resources/Data/triggers.json` until the owner has edited this list.

How triggers work in the game (owner's design): the character has triggers. The player learns them over time
(first a hint, then the full name) and tries to avoid them. When one fires, it causes unwanted
**depersonalization, derealization or a switch**, and the player has **no control** over which. The game shows
the *cue*, never the trauma behind it.

Each idea has: the **cue** (what the scene emits), a **hint** (shown the first time), and the **suggested
outcomes**. For each idea, the owner decides **keep / change / cut**, which outcomes and how likely,
and whether it affects **everyone** or only **certain alters**.

## Design notes
- Several religious cues appear naturally in the chapters (bells, candles, silence). That tension is
  intentional: exploring a faith can mean walking towards a trigger. The player learns where the risks are and can
  choose routes around them (the game tracks avoidance gently, never as a score).
- Shabbat candles and meeting-house silence are also beautiful parts of those traditions. The story should
  hold both: the trigger belongs to the character's history, not to the tradition.
- Comfort settings still apply for real players (caps on visual distortion and camera drift), and "ask before
  involuntary switches" remains an accessibility option (off by default), so the in-story lack of control is
  kept unless a player needs it.
