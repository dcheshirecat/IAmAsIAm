## What a UU congregation is like
- **No creed.** A congregation doesn't agree on beliefs. It makes a *covenant*, a promise about how members will treat each
  other. Members can be humanists, Christians, Buddhists, Jews, pagans, or several at once.
- **Shared values.** In 2024 the Unitarian Universalist Association adopted new shared values with Love at the centre:
  Justice, Equity, Transformation, Pluralism, Interdependence and Generosity. The older Seven Principles are still widely
  known, and the chapter intro quotes the first one.
- **A Sunday service** usually includes:
  - lighting the **flaming chalice** (the UU symbol);
  - "**joys and concerns**", when people light a candle and share news;
  - readings from many traditions;
  - **coffee hour** afterwards.
- **Small groups** inside a congregation: Buddhist sitting groups, humanist book clubs, Jewish UU gatherings, and pagan
  circles.
- **CUUPS** (the Covenant of Unitarian Universalist Pagans) chapters are pagan groups inside UU congregations, led by UUs.
  Many mark all eight sabbats, sometimes on the nearest Sunday or Saturday.
- **The Flower Communion** was created by Norbert Čapek, founder of the Unitarian church in Prague, in 1923. Everyone brings
  a flower, the flowers go into one vase, and everyone takes home a different one. Many UU congregations hold it in late
  spring.
- **Winter solstice services** are common in UU congregations, as are solstice rituals in their pagan circles.
