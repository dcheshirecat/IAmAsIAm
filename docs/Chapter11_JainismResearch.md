## Core ideas, as shown
- **Ahimsa:** non-harm to every living being (jiva). It's why many Jains:
  - filter water;
  - eat before sunset, so they don't swallow tiny flying insects;
  - avoid root vegetables, since pulling one up kills the whole plant and the small lives in the soil;
  - sweep softly in front of them, so as not to step on anything.

  Nirmala-ben turns it inward: the first jiva you are responsible for is the one you live in.
- **Aparigraha:** non-attachment, holding on lightly.
- **Anekantavada:** many-sidedness. No one sees the whole truth, as in the parable of the blind men and the elephant.
- **Streams:** Svetambara, Digambara, Sthanakvasi and Terapanth. In North America they often share one centre, with separate
  schedules. Some Svetambara Jains hold a small white cloth (muhpatti) at the mouth during prayer, to protect tiny beings in the
  air.
- **Visiting a temple (derasar):** shoes and leather stay outside. The images of the Tirthankaras are seated or standing, eyes
  half closed.
- **Paryushana:** Svetambara Jains keep it for eight days; Digambara Jains keep a ten-day version (Das Lakshana). The last day
  is **Samvatsari**, with fasting and the long evening **pratikraman** (a ritual of reflection and repentance). Afterwards,
  everyone asks everyone's forgiveness: **"Micchami Dukkadam"**. One verse said there: "Khamemi savve jiva, savve jiva khamantu
  me, mitti me savva bhuesu, veram majjha na kenai" (I forgive all living beings; may all beings forgive me; I am friends with
  all and enemies with none). Calendar listings give 2034 dates of about September 10 to 17.
- **Diwali:** for Jains it marks Mahavira's nirvana, his final liberation, at Pavapuri. Lamps stand for the light of his
  knowledge, and the next day is the Jain New Year. Calendar listings put Diwali 2034 at about November 10.
