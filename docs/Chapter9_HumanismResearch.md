# Chapter 9: Humanism, research notes

Owner's choices: a fresh start in a real, foggy coastal town in Northern California; the guide is **Miss Ella** (30, she/her),
a teacher who was raised religious, left, and built an after-school club because nobody did it for her; the heart of the
chapter is **Maisie** (13, she/her), who goes quiet and "floaty" and doesn't answer her name. Starts as Heather (who signed
up to volunteer); unlocks nobody. One school year, 2031-32.

## The town: Arcata, California
Why Arcata: it matches every part of the brief. It's a small college town on Humboldt Bay, often in fog, with the redwoods
right at the edge of town, and it has a small natural history museum where Maya, a museum educator, can work.
- **The Plaza** is the town's centre: a grassy square with a statue, surrounded by old shopfronts and cafés. It holds the
  farmers' market and festivals. Arcata Chamber of Commerce guides call it the community's living room.
- **The Arcata Marsh and Wildlife Sanctuary** lies on the bay side of town. It has walking trails and boardwalks, and it's a
  birdwatching spot (egrets, pelicans). Guided walks run on Saturdays, and the marsh is part of the town's wastewater system.
- **The Arcata Community Forest** is a city-owned redwood forest (about 793 acres) just east of downtown. It was dedicated
  in 1955 as the first city-owned community forest in California.
- **The Natural History Museum of Cal Poly Humboldt** (1242 G Street) runs school programs, kids' camps and family days,
  and has a fossil collection of over 2,000 specimens. Placing Maya there as an educator is Claude's guess.
- **Whose land:** Arcata is on Wiyot land. The Wiyot name for the place is **Goudi'ni** ("over in the woods"); the English
  name comes from the Yurok word *oket'oh* (the lagoon). In 1860 settlers massacred Wiyot people at Tuluwat, on an island in
  the bay. The island has since been returned to the Wiyot Tribe in stages (completed in 2019; sources differ on 2018 or
  2019), and the tribe resumed its World Renewal Ceremony there in 2014. The game shows only the museum's acknowledgement
  sign; it doesn't depict Wiyot religion (not ours to show).
- **The school is not named.** Real schools shouldn't be given made-up staff, so the game calls it "the middle school".

## Humanism, as shown
- **What it is:** a non-religious way of living that relies on reason, compassion and human dignity. Humanists believe
  this life is the only one we know about, so we're responsible for it and for each other. Miss Ella's version: "It fits on a
  sticky note."
- **Darwin Day (February 12),** Charles Darwin's birthday, is celebrated by many humanist and secular groups as a day for
  science and curiosity. In Part 2, Maya runs one at the museum.
- **World Humanist Day (June 21)** is mentioned in Part 3.
- **Humanist celebrants** lead non-religious weddings, funerals and namings. Miss Ella is training to become one.
- **The variety shown:** Miss Ella left a strict church; a kid at the club says grace over his crackers and nobody laughs;
  Maya keeps her many beliefs and adds "the plain one". Humanism here is open to everyone's beliefs; it doesn't sneer at them.
- **Local groups:** a search found no secular humanist group, Sunday Assembly or humanist celebrant based in Arcata or
  Eureka, so the game doesn't invent one. The World Humanist Day picnic on the Plaza is Claude's placeholder.

## Speaking up (the heart of the chapter)
Maya doesn't diagnose Maisie or try to fix her. She keeps showing up, says Maisie's name, gives her something real to hold
(a fossil), and then tells the teacher what she has seen. Miss Ella takes it to the school counsellor and to Maisie's
grandmother. That's how a volunteer should act on a worry about a child, and it's exactly what Mira couldn't do in Prologue
Sunday 2.
